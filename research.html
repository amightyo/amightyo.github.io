Research

Data, Intelligence, and Evidence for Human-Centered Impact

My research sits at the intersection of data science, artificial intelligence, education, health, and advanced quantitative methods.

Across these areas, I am interested in a common question:

How can data, artificial intelligence, and rigorous analytical methods be used to generate better evidence, support better decisions, and improve human outcomes?

My research program is organized around four interconnected streams:

  1. AI, Data Science & Education
  2. Health & Wellness Analytics
  3. Advanced Quantitative Methods
  4. Science of Research & Emerging AI

These streams reflect my interdisciplinary background in engineering, computer science, instructional technology, analytics, and academic leadership. They also provide the intellectual foundation for the emerging INSIGHT Lab.

AI, Data Science & Education

Artificial intelligence is increasingly shaping how people learn, work, communicate, and make decisions. My research in this area examines both the opportunities created by AI and the human, educational, and organizational factors that influence its effective use.

I am particularly interested in moving beyond questions of whether people use artificial intelligence toward understanding how, why, and under what conditions AI contributes to meaningful outcomes.

Areas of Interest

  • Artificial intelligence literacy
  • Predictive AI and machine learning
  • Generative AI
  • Agentic AI
  • Human–AI collaboration
  • AI adoption and attitudes
  • Trust in artificial intelligence
  • AI-supported decision-making
  • Learning analytics
  • Student engagement and academic success
  • Responsible AI use in education
  • Faculty and student adoption of emerging technologies

Questions That Motivate This Work

  • What factors influence people’s attitudes toward and adoption of artificial intelligence?
  • How does AI literacy shape effective and responsible use of AI?
  • When does human–AI collaboration improve decision quality?
  • How can predictive AI support educational decision-making without reinforcing existing inequities?
  • What distinguishes productive AI assistance from overreliance on AI?
  • How should educators redesign learning and assessment in response to generative and agentic AI?
  • How can AI support students while preserving critical thinking and intellectual independence?

Current and Emerging Directions

My current work in this stream includes research examining student attitudes toward artificial intelligence, AI literacy and readiness, personality and attitudes toward AI, human–AI decision-making, and the role of emerging AI technologies in education and organizations.

This research increasingly considers AI not simply as a technological tool, but as part of a broader human–technology system in which outcomes depend on the interaction among people, technology, context, and decision processes.

Health & Wellness Analytics

My research in health and wellness analytics uses data science, epidemiological reasoning, and advanced quantitative methods to investigate factors associated with individual and population health.

I am particularly interested in health questions where multiple behavioral, psychological, and contextual factors interact to influence outcomes.

Areas of Interest

  • Health informatics
  • Sleep quality and sleep duration
  • Physical activity
  • Sedentary behavior
  • Metabolic health
  • Psychological wellness
  • Population health
  • Epidemiology
  • Public health analytics
  • Health disparities
  • Predictive health analytics
  • AI-enabled health decision support

Questions That Motivate This Work

  • How do sleep, physical activity, and other health behaviors interact to influence long-term health?
  • Which health indicators provide the most meaningful information for identifying risk?
  • How can large public datasets be used to generate actionable population health insights?
  • How can predictive analytics support earlier identification of health risks?
  • How should uncertainty be incorporated into health-related decision-making?
  • What role can artificial intelligence play in supporting clinicians, patients, and health systems?

Current and Emerging Directions

My recent work has examined relationships among sleep, metabolic health, physical activity, psychological wellness, and health risk using population-level data.

I am particularly interested in expanding this work through public datasets and reproducible analytical workflows that combine epidemiology, Bayesian methods, structural equation modeling, and predictive analytics.

The long-term goal is to contribute to a more integrated understanding of health and wellness in which analytical models support prevention, decision-making, and meaningful human outcomes.

Advanced Quantitative Methods

Methodology is a central part of my research identity.

I am interested not only in applying statistical and computational methods, but also in helping researchers select methods that appropriately reflect their theoretical questions, data structures, and inferential goals.

My methodological work spans explanatory, predictive, and evidence-synthesis approaches.

Areas of Interest

  • Structural equation modeling
  • Confirmatory factor analysis
  • Bayesian statistics
  • Bayesian model comparison
  • Predictive modeling
  • Machine learning
  • Meta-analysis
  • Multivariate analysis
  • Regression modeling
  • Longitudinal and multilevel methods
  • Latent variable modeling
  • Data visualization

Questions That Motivate This Work

  • Which analytical method is most appropriate for the research question being asked?
  • How can researchers distinguish explanatory modeling from predictive modeling?
  • How can uncertainty be communicated more effectively in quantitative research?
  • When do latent variable models provide a better representation of complex constructs?
  • How can Bayesian methods improve evidence-based decision-making?
  • How can multiple studies be synthesized to estimate the overall strength and consistency of evidence?
  • How can advanced methods remain rigorous while also being understandable to applied researchers and decision-makers?

Methodological Philosophy

I view analytical methods as tools for answering substantive questions rather than as ends in themselves.

My approach emphasizes alignment among:

Theory → Research Question → Data → Method → Evidence → Interpretation

This perspective also informs my doctoral mentorship. I encourage researchers to begin with the problem and theoretical framework before selecting an analytical technique.

Science of Research & Emerging AI

As artificial intelligence changes how research is discovered, conducted, analyzed, and communicated, I have become increasingly interested in studying the research process itself.

This research stream examines the structure and evolution of scientific knowledge while also exploring how emerging AI technologies may transform research workflows.

Areas of Interest

  • Bibliometric analysis
  • Science mapping
  • Research synthesis
  • Meta-analysis
  • Open science
  • Reproducible research
  • Computational literature analysis
  • Retrieval-augmented generation
  • Agentic AI
  • AI-assisted research workflows
  • Research transparency
  • Scholarly communication

Questions That Motivate This Work

  • How do new research fields emerge and evolve?
  • Who and what shape the intellectual structure of a research domain?
  • How can bibliometric methods identify emerging themes and research gaps?
  • How can research synthesis become more transparent and reproducible?
  • How can artificial intelligence responsibly support literature discovery and evidence synthesis?
  • How will retrieval-augmented generation and agentic AI change scientific research?
  • What safeguards are needed when AI becomes increasingly integrated into research workflows?

Current and Emerging Directions

Current work in this area includes research on the evolution of retrieval-augmented generation for large language models, human–AI decision-making, and the development of robust and reproducible bibliometric workflows.

I am particularly interested in integrating tools and data sources such as OpenAlex, Bibliometrix, VOSviewer, R, Python, GitHub, and Quarto to create transparent research pipelines that can be reused and extended by other researchers.

A longer-term direction of this work is to examine how AI agents and human researchers may collaborate across the research lifecycle, from literature discovery and hypothesis generation to analysis, reproducibility, and scholarly communication.

Connecting the Research Streams

Although these four streams are presented separately, they are intentionally interconnected.

For example:

  • Research on AI in education requires understanding human behavior, learning, and responsible technology adoption.
  • Research on health analytics benefits from advanced statistical methods and interpretable predictive models.
  • Research on human–AI decision-making connects artificial intelligence with psychology, organizations, health, and education.
  • Research on bibliometrics and meta-analysis strengthens our ability to understand accumulated scientific evidence.
  • Research on reproducibility and emerging AI influences how scholarship across all of these areas is conducted.

Together, these streams form a broader research agenda focused on the responsible use of data, intelligence, and evidence to understand complex systems and improve human outcomes.

Research Approach

Across my research, I emphasize several guiding principles.

Interdisciplinary Thinking

Complex problems often require perspectives and methods from multiple disciplines.

Methodological Rigor

Research questions, theory, data, and analytical methods should be carefully aligned.

Reproducibility

Research workflows should be transparent and reproducible whenever possible.

Human-Centered AI

Technological innovation should be evaluated in relation to the people, communities, and systems it affects.

Open and Collaborative Science

Research is strengthened through collaboration, transparency, and the responsible sharing of knowledge.

Mentorship

Developing the next generation of researchers is an important part of scholarly impact.
