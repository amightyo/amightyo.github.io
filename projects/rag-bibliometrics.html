Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Large Language Models

Mapping the Evolution and Intellectual Structure of RAG Research

Global Research Trends in Retrieval-Augmented Generation

A Bibliometric Analysis of RAG for Large Language Models

Bibliometrics Artificial Intelligence Open Science

Status: Ongoing Research

Research Stream: Science of Research & Emerging AI

Methods: Bibliometric Analysis · Science Mapping · Computational Text Analysis

Project Overview

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) has emerged as an important approach for extending the capabilities of large language models by integrating information retrieval with generative artificial intelligence.

As research in this area expands rapidly, there is a need to systematically examine how the field has developed, who and what have shaped its intellectual structure, and which themes are emerging.

This project uses bibliometric analysis and science mapping to examine the evolution of research on retrieval-augmented generation for large language models.

The study is designed not only to describe publication growth, but also to investigate the intellectual, conceptual, and collaborative structures shaping the field.

Scope of the Study

The primary unit of analysis is:

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) for Large Language Models (LLMs)

This scope intentionally focuses on the modern RAG paradigm associated with large language models rather than broadly combining it with earlier retrieval-based generation approaches in traditional natural language processing.

Earlier retrieval-generation methods may provide important historical context, but they are conceptually distinguished from the primary unit of analysis.

The current study period focuses on research published through 2025, allowing the analysis to examine the emergence and rapid growth of the field while using a complete publication year as the endpoint.

Research Questions

The project is guided by the following broad research questions:

RQ1 — Publication Trends

How has scholarly research on retrieval-augmented generation for large language models evolved over time?

RQ2 — Research Influence

Who are the most influential authors, institutions, countries, publications, and sources contributing to RAG research?

RQ3 — Collaboration

What patterns of collaboration characterize the global RAG research community?

RQ4 — Intellectual Structure

What citation and knowledge structures define the intellectual foundations of RAG research?

RQ5 — Emerging Themes

What major themes, research clusters, and emerging topics characterize the development of RAG for large language models?

Search Strategy

A central methodological consideration in this project is how to identify relevant RAG research comprehensively.

Searching only for the exact phrase “retrieval-augmented generation” may exclude relevant studies that describe similar architectures, approaches, or mechanisms using different terminology.

The project therefore uses a broader metadata-based retrieval strategy.

Records are retrieved using relevant information available through:

  • Titles
  • Abstracts
  • Keywords
  • Concepts and topics
  • Related metadata fields

The search incorporates a comprehensive set of terms associated with retrieval-augmented generation and large language models.

Retrieved records are subsequently screened for relevance according to predefined eligibility criteria.

This approach is designed to improve recall while reducing unnecessary selection bias introduced by relying on a single exact phrase.

Data Source

The primary bibliographic data source for this project is OpenAlex.

OpenAlex provides open scholarly metadata that can support:

  • Collaboration networks

The use of an open bibliographic data source also aligns with the project’s broader commitment to reproducible and transparent research.

Research Workflow

The project follows a reproducible bibliometric workflow:

Define Scope → Develop Search Strategy → Retrieve Records → Screen for Relevance → Clean Metadata → Analyze → Visualize → Interpret → Reproduce

1. Define the Unit of Analysis

Clearly define RAG for LLMs as the primary conceptual domain.

2. Develop the Search Strategy

Construct a comprehensive metadata search strategy designed to balance precision and recall.

3. Retrieve Bibliographic Records

Retrieve scholarly metadata through OpenAlex.

4. Screen Records

Manually screen retrieved records using predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria.

5. Clean and Standardize Metadata

Address issues such as:

  • Duplicate records
  • Author name variants
  • Institutional name variants
  • Keyword inconsistencies
  • Missing metadata

6. Conduct Bibliometric Analysis

Analyze publication growth, productivity, impact, collaboration, citation structures, and research themes.

7. Conduct Science Mapping

Develop network-based representations of the field.

8. Analyze Emerging Themes

Use keyword analysis and computational text methods to identify developing research topics.

9. Document the Workflow

Maintain transparent documentation of search, screening, cleaning, and analytical decisions.

Computational Text Analysis

In addition to traditional bibliometric methods, the project explores computational text analysis to identify meaningful terminology within the literature.

TF-IDF

Term Frequency–Inverse Document Frequency (TF-IDF) may be used to identify terms that are particularly informative within documents or groups of documents.

This can complement author-provided keywords and bibliometric concept metadata by identifying terminology directly from titles and abstracts.

The integration of bibliometric and text-analytic approaches may provide a richer understanding of how RAG research themes have evolved.

Broader Research Significance

RAG represents an important development in the evolution of large language models because it connects generative systems with external sources of information.

Understanding the development of this research domain can provide insight into:

  • How the field has evolved
  • Which researchers and institutions are shaping its development
  • How international collaboration is structured
  • Which intellectual foundations influence current research
  • Which topics are becoming more prominent
  • Where future research opportunities may exist
